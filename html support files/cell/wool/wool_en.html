Level 1: from the Road north / Road south edge entrances along high-weight wool corridors into the centre.　Level 2: merges the former secondary and local access roads; handles shared/hub distribution, parcel service and valid service ends. Internal strength and continuous length still control quality, but all are shown as level 2.　Level 2 roads also give local access and parcel service: unserved parcels are found by the distance from the parcel edge to real road segments, then joined along the wool graph. Auto-connection and 'short aligned gap bridging' are both strictly limited by 'Road repair distance' and never connect beyond it; a short gap is closed only if it is aligned, the bridge lies inside the site and it does not cross a parcel interior. Only roads connected to the level 1 root network are output, and isolated blue lines are removed automatically. Formal ends are classed as 'entrance / valid L2 service end / bridgeable gap / faulty dead end': L1 may end only at a formal entrance; L2 handles both secondary distribution and local access, and may form a service end only where it truly serves a parcel, joins the main network and has no sensible road to connect to nearby; other faulty dead ends are bridged automatically first, and end branches that still cannot be repaired are removed. Graph node welding and anchor snapping each use their own small tolerance in metres and detect real X / T junctions; the final connectivity check also detects crossings mid-segment, so real intersecting roads are not deleted by mistake.
